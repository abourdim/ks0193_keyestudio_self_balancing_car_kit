Before you touch anything — the four things that ruin a first session

🔌

Unplug the blue Bluetooth board before EVERY upload

It sits on the same two wires the USB cable uses. Leave it in and the upload fails with a confusing error. Pull it out, upload, push it back in. You will forget this at least once.

🔋

The battery switch must be ON

USB powers the brain but not the motors. If your program runs and the wheels stay dead, this is almost always why.

✋

Hold the car still and upright when you switch it on

In its first second the car measures which way is up. Move it during that second and it learns the wrong answer — and then falls over all session.

📦

Wheels off the table

From step 9 on, the motors start the instant it powers up. Stand the car on a box, or hold it. Never flash it lying on the floor next to your foot.

How to run any step. Open a terminal in this folder and type ./launch.sh. Pick the step number, then choose 5 to send it to the car, or 7 to read what the car is saying. That is the whole tool.

08

Kalman filter

Two flawed sensors, one good answer.

  2. Do the exact same tap test as step 7.
  3. Compare the two numbers on screen.
👀 You should see

Same sensor, same tap. The difference is entirely in the maths.

🔧 Not working?
  • Both numbers jump → you are still running step 7.
predict with the gyro correct with the accelerometer constants = how much you trust each it learns the gyro's drift
The whole idea, in three sentences

You have two informants. One is jumpy but honest: ask him where you are and he shouts a different answer every second, but on average he is right. The other is calm but a liar: he tells you smoothly how much you just turned, and he is superb at it — except he lies by a tiny constant amount, and those tiny lies pile up until you are completely lost.

The filter does what a sensible person would do. Follow the calm one second by second. Use the jumpy one to slowly fix your position. And — the clever bit — keep a running note of exactly how much the calm one lies, so you can subtract it.

The two informants each one is useless alone, and they fail in opposite ways

1 When two people tell you different things, who do you believe?

That percentage has a name: the Kalman gain. It is not a setting you choose — the filter recomputes it every 5 ms from how sure it currently is.

2 Guessing makes you less sure. Looking makes you more sure.

The cloud is how unsure the filter is. Watch it breathe: it puffs up on every PREDICT and gets squeezed on every CORRECT. That breathing is the entire P[2][2] matrix in the code.

3 The clever bit: it works out how much the gyro lies● live

The car is perfectly still the whole time. The violet line is what you get by simply adding up the gyro. The cyan line is the filter. The number it discovers is q_bias — one line of code, line 97.

4 All of it together — the same real tilt, measured three ways● live

Drag the slider to the far left and the output follows the gyro and slides away. Far right and it copies the accelerometer's noise. The kit sits near the middle.

ConstantValueMeans
Q_angle0.001we assume little noise in the gyro prediction → trust it

R_angle is 500× Q_angle. That ratio is the whole idea: follow the gyro moment to moment, let the accelerometer nudge it slowly back to truth.

The code
angle    += (gyro_m-q_bias)*dt;
angle_err = angle_m - angle;
// ...covariance -> K_0, K_1
angle    += K_0 * angle_err;
q_bias   += K_1 * angle_err;
Pins
Open the engine — every line of Kalman_Filter(), explained

It tracks two numbers, not one. angle (the tilt) and q_bias (how much the gyro lies). That is why P is a 2×2 matrix: one uncertainty for each.

LineCodeIn plain words
67angle += (gyro_m - q_bias)*dtPredict. Move the estimate at the rate the gyro reports, minus the lie we have learned so far.
68angle_err = angle_m - angleHow much the accelerometer disagrees. This one number drives every correction below.
70–78Pdot[…], P[…] += Pdot*dtGet less sure. This is Ṗ = A·P + P·Aᵀ + Q written out by hand. Pure guessing costs confidence.
84E = R_angle + P[0][0]My uncertainty + the sensor's noise. The denominator of the gain.
86–87K_0 = P[0][0]/EThe gain. Sure of myself → K≈0, ignore the accelerometer. Lost → K≈1, jump to it.
92–95P -= K*C*PGet more sure. A measurement arrived; shrink the cloud.
97q_bias += K_1*angle_errLearn the lie. A disagreement that never goes away can only be gyro drift.
98angle_speed = gyro_m - q_biasThe de-biased rate — this is what lesson 09's D term uses.
99angle += K_0*angle_errCorrect. Move part of the way toward the accelerometer.
The D term is not a derivative. Line 98 hands lesson 09 the gyro's own rate measurement, bias-corrected. Nothing in this kit ever differentiates a noisy signal — the gyroscope supplies the derivative for free. That is exactly why a PD controller is realistic on a 16 MHz chip.
Two oddities worth spotting. angle_calculate() is handed six parameters (dt, Q_angle, …) and uses none of them — the filter reads the globals instead. And K1 = 0.05 is declared, passed around, and never used anywhere. Same behaviour, but it is dead weight.
In lesson 08, dt is a lie. This sketch has no MsTimer2 — loop() runs free, and four Serial.print calls at 9600 baud take tens of milliseconds on their own. So the real period is nothing like the 5 ms the code assumes. It still smooths beautifully, which is enough for a demo, but the filter is only properly scaled from lesson 09, where it is called from the 5 ms interrupt. That is the best possible argument for lesson 04.

The gains settle. After a few seconds P stops changing, so K_0 and K_1 become almost constant. From then on this behaves like a fixed-blend filter plus bias learning — which is why many projects compute the two gains once and delete all the matrix algebra.

Why it is the hinge of the whole kit

The controller in lesson 09 uses a derivative term, and differentiating a noisy signal explodes the noise. Feed it the raw accelerometer angle and the motors scream and jitter without ever balancing. A clean angle is not a nicety — it is the precondition.

Try it to go somewhere, a balancing robot must first lean

The key idea. The speed loop's output does not go to the motors. It shifts the target angle of the angle loop. To stop rolling forward, the car is told to lean back a little — and the angle loop does the real work.
Why I and not D. Drift is a small, persistent error — exactly what an integral term is for. A derivative would differentiate the encoder count, which moves in whole pulses; differentiating a staircase gives spikes. Hence kd_speed = 0. Each loop uses only the terms its signal can support.

Why it is last, and not first

Zero degrees on the MPU6050 is where the sensor chip is flat. That is not where this chassis balances: battery position, how the sensor board was screwed on, wheel wear, a slightly bent axle — each shifts the real equilibrium by a degree or two.

And a degree or two is a lot. The angle loop drives faithfully toward whatever target it was given, so a wrong target means the car correctly holds the wrong posture — and a leaning car accelerates.

What happens next depends on how much of the machine you have built. With the angle loop alone — lesson 09 — nothing absorbs the error, so it rolls away, hits the PWM limit and falls. That is the wrong balance point preset in the lesson 12 playground.

Once the speed loop exists, something subtler happens, and you can watch it in the simulator. A 3° error does not topple the car: the speed loop quietly cancels it by parking the car a couple of metres from where you left it, holding a permanent lean, looking perfectly happy. The integral term is buying off your calibration error with position. But it can only buy so much — positions is clamped at ±3550, so beyond roughly 8° there is nothing left to give and the car leaves for good. Both presets are there: zero off by 3° and zero off by 9°.
