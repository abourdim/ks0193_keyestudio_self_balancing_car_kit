Porting to ESP32-S3

Replacing the UNO under the balance shield with a D1 ESP32-S3 (WR-OM-1, N8R2/N16R8) — a UNO-footprint board with CH340C, AMS1117 and a 5–18 V jack. It fits the shield mechanically. It is not a drop-in electrically or in software.

Read this before you buy the level shifters

The GPIO column below is transcribed from the seller's pinout diagram, not measured from a board. Meter the shield against the header before you cut, jumper or order anything — every step on this page depends on that mapping being right.

Four hardware problems

1 · The I²C pins are no longer the same pins

On a UNO R3, A4 is SDA and A5 is SCL — one net, two header positions. On this board they are four separate GPIOs: A4/A5 land on IO1/IO2, while the R3 SDA/SCL positions are IO8/IO9. Find which route the shield's MPU6050 uses with a continuity test, then pass that pair explicitly:

Wire.begin(SDA_PIN, SCL_PIN);   // no A4/A5 convention here

2 · Two motor lines land on strapping pins

left_L2→IO3 and PWM_L→IO46 both influence how the ESP32-S3 comes out of reset. If the shield holds either one during boot, the board may enter the wrong boot mode or refuse to start. This is the failure most likely to cost you an evening, and it looks like a dead board rather than a pin conflict. The usual fix is to cut those two header traces and jumper both functions to free GPIOs.

3 · 5 V encoder outputs into 3.3 V inputs

The ESP32-S3 is not 5 V tolerant. The hall encoder outputs go straight into IO19/IO20, so a divider or level shifter is mandatory, not a nicety. The motor driver direction is the easy way round — TB6612 inputs accept 3.3 V logic fine.

4 · The encoder pins are the USB pair

IO19/IO20 are USB D−/D+ on ESP32-S3. They work as ordinary GPIO provided the board's USB socket really is wired to the CH340C and native USB stays disabled. Confirm that from the board itself — if that port turns out to be native USB, you cannot have both USB and your encoders.

The Bluetooth upload conflict does not go away

The CH340C sits on U0 (GPIO43/44), the same UART the D0/D1 header exposes — so the module in the XBee socket still fights the USB bridge exactly as it does on the UNO. It would only be solved by native USB-CDC, which needs IO19/IO20, which are your encoders. On this board you likely cannot have both.

The I2Cdev and MPU6050 libraries talk through Wire and should build; the Kalman maths is plain float and gets faster on the S3's FPU. Only the two AVR-specific libraries are hard blockers.

What to do, in order

  1. Confirm the pin map. Meter the shield against the header. Everything above is downstream of this.
  2. Sort the I²C route, then run lesson 06 (06_MPU6050). It needs only Wire — no timers, no interrupts, no motors. Raw MPU6050 numbers arriving means board and bus are good.
  3. Lesson 02, then 03 — motors, then encoders with the dividers fitted. These flush out the strapping-pin problem while the car is safely on a stand.
  4. Lesson 04 — the replacement timer, on its own, before anything depends on it.
  5. Then 08 → 09 → 10, and budget real time for retuning.

Lessons 01–08 are the cheap part. 09–13 are where the retuning cost lives.
